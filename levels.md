# Netflix Software Engineer Levels

Netflix engineer levels are commonly reported to run from L3 to L7. Most engineers join at L4 or L5, and L5, the senior level, is the level that most Netflix engineers hold. The interview is calibrated to senior work, so the level decision depends on scope and independence more than on coding skill.

These levels come from job postings and candidate reports. Netflix does not publish a detailed level matrix, and some offers are reported to arrive with no level number.

## The ladder

| Level | Title | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **L3** | Software Engineer (entry) | Well-defined tasks inside one team, with regular review |
| **L4** | Software Engineer | Features built end to end with little supervision |
| **L5** | Senior Software Engineer | A significant area owned end to end with very little supervision |
| **L6** | Staff Software Engineer | Work that spans several teams |
| **L7** | Principal Software Engineer | Technical direction for an organization of many teams |

## Why the ladder is senior-heavy

Netflix hires experienced people and gives them very little supervision. Its culture documents describe this as freedom and responsibility. That model suits engineers who already work independently, so roles for engineers with under two years of experience are rare.

Three facts follow for a candidate.

- L3 exists mainly for a small early career program, and many teams have no L3 engineers.
- L5 is effectively a terminal level, which is a level where an engineer can stay for a whole career without promotion pressure.
- L6 and L7 are reported to be rarer at Netflix than the same levels at other large companies.

## L4 or L5

Most candidates are deciding between these two levels. The difference is scope, not coding skill.

| Question | L4 | L5 |
|---|---|---|
| What do you own? | A component | An area or a significant system |
| Who decides what to build? | Usually someone else | You, inside your area |
| How far does the work reach? | One team | Across team boundaries when the problem needs it |

## How the interview changes by level

| Level | What the loop emphasizes |
|---|---|
| L3 | Coding fundamentals and the ability to learn; few openings exist |
| L4 | Working code and clear reasoning, plus more independence than a mid-level role elsewhere |
| L5 | Architecture and judgment over algorithm puzzles; system design often appears twice |
| L6 and L7 | Direction across several teams, and a long record of delivered work |

The rounds that examine experience change the most. Senior candidates should expect a long discussion of two or three past projects, to a depth of four follow-up questions. Know the numbers, the constraints, and what you would change now.

Mid-level candidates are reported to pass every technical round and still receive no offer. In those reports the gap is independence: decisions made alone, scope defined without help, and work stopped because it was not worth doing.

## How pay is structured

This guide gives no pay figures. The structure is still worth knowing, because it differs from other large companies. Netflix is reported to pay one yearly amount with no separate performance bonus. Each employee chooses how to divide that amount between salary and stock options. Pay is also reported to move within a level, not only at promotion.

## Comparing levels

The Netflix numbers are reported to match Google numbers closely from L3 to L7. Any mapping to another company is approximate. Ask the recruiter what the role owns before you compare an offer.
