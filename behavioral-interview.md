# Netflix Behavioral Interview

Netflix frames its behavioral interviews around a public culture memo, and interviewers ask questions that map to it directly. The culture conversations are commonly reported to decide the result alone, because strong technical rounds do not cancel a weak culture result. Preparation therefore starts with reading the memo and forming an honest opinion about it.

## The culture memo

The culture memo is a document that Netflix publishes to describe how its employees are expected to work. DesignGurus.io sources report the following content.

| Part of the memo | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Four principles | The dream team, people over process, uncomfortably exciting, and great and always better |
| Eight values | Selflessness, judgment, candor, creativity, courage, inclusion, curiosity, and resilience |
| Freedom and responsibility | Wide autonomy, with the expectation that each person acts in the company's interest |
| The keeper test | A manager asks: would I fight to keep this person if they wanted to leave? |

Candor means giving and receiving direct feedback.

## How the round differs

- **Candor is tested during the conversation.** Interviewers notice answers that avoid the difficult part of a story.
- **Judgment is examined.** Netflix gives engineers wide autonomy, so questions ask how you decide when nobody checks your work.
- **The bar assumes an experienced professional.** Stories should show you working independently, not learning a role with heavy support.
- **The memo itself is a topic.** Expect to be asked what you took from it and what you disagree with.

## Question themes

| Theme | Example question |
|---|---|
| Culture memo and motivation | What did you take away from our culture memo, and what do you disagree with? |
| Candor given | Tell me about the hardest feedback you have given someone. |
| Candor received | Tell me about feedback that was hard to hear. What did you do with it? |
| Judgment and autonomy | Tell me about a significant decision you made without asking permission. |
| Selflessness | Tell me about a time you helped another team at a cost to your own goals. |
| Courage | Describe an unpopular position you took. Did you change anyone's mind? |

More questions, including "Why Netflix?", are in the [question bank](question-bank.md).

## How to structure a story

Use STAR, which stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. The situation gives the context in two sentences. The task states what you were responsible for. The action describes what you did, and the result states the outcome with a number.

Netflix answers need three additions to a standard STAR answer.

1. **The uncomfortable part.** Keep the moment when your feedback was received badly or when criticism hurt. That moment makes the story credible.
2. **The reasoning.** For a judgment story, name the decision, the money or risk involved, the information you had, and why you did not escalate.
3. **The lesson.** End with what you would do differently now.

## Stories to prepare

Prepare at least four stories, one for each theme below.

- A judgment call you made without asking permission.
- Feedback you gave that was hard to give.
- Feedback you received and acted on.
- A choice that helped the company more than it helped you.

Also prepare three short answers.

- One value from the memo that you have lived, with the story that proves it.
- One honest disagreement with the memo, stated respectfully.
- Why a manager would fight to keep you.

A calm and honest tone is reported to work better than a show of intensity.
