# Netflix Interview Process

The Netflix software engineer interview is commonly reported as four main stages: a recruiter call, a hiring manager screen, a technical screen, and a virtual onsite loop of four to six interviews. Most candidates report four to eight weeks from the first call to a decision. Teams run their own hiring, so the exact stages differ by team.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Application review | Resume and any referral; one to two weeks is reported | Whether your experience fits the role |
| Recruiter screen | A call of about 30 minutes | Whether your background, motivation, and level fit the opening |
| Hiring manager screen | 30 to 60 minutes | Whether your past work and decisions match the team |
| Technical screen | 45 to 60 minutes in a shared editor | Whether you can write working code and explain it |
| Take-home project (some teams) | A practical task done in your own time | Whether your code holds up at realistic size |
| Virtual onsite loop | Four to six interviews, sometimes across two days | System design, coding, and values and judgment |
| Director conversations (some loops) | About 45 minutes each | Judgment and fit beyond one team |
| References and decision | A recruiter call or email | Offer or no offer |

Not every candidate sees every stage. Some teams replace the take-home project with a second system design interview.

## What each stage is like

**Recruiter and hiring manager screens.** These two calls are conversations, not tests. The recruiter asks about your background, the work you want next, and why you chose Netflix. The hiring manager asks about recent projects and the decisions you made. Compensation is reported to come up earlier than at most companies, so decide what you will say beforehand.

**Technical screen.** The screen is practical and often tied to the team's own work. See the [coding interview](coding-interview.md) page.

**Onsite loop.** Candidates report four to six interviews, and recruiting often offers to split them across two days. The loop mixes [system design](system-design-interview.md), coding, and [culture conversations](behavioral-interview.md) with future peers. Senior candidates often face system design twice. Every interview ends with time for your own questions.

**Director conversations.** Some loops add one or two directors. Candidates report one director from the hiring team and one from a partner team. The stated purpose is a wider view of the same candidate.

## Who decides

One DesignGurus.io guide reports that Netflix has no separate hiring committee, which is a group that reviews interview feedback without meeting the candidate. In that report, the interviewers you meet make the decision. The practical effect is that each conversation counts, including the culture conversations.

## Timeline

| Step | Commonly reported wait |
|---|---|
| Application to first contact | One to two weeks |
| Scheduling the onsite loop | Two to three weeks |
| Final round to decision | A few days to about two weeks |
| Whole process | Four to eight weeks; some candidates report under three |

If one week passes after the final round with no update, send one short message to the recruiter. Ask about status and next steps.

## After the decision

- References are commonly requested late, after the main interviews, when the team is considering an offer.
- Rejections usually arrive as a short note from the recruiter, and detailed feedback is rare.
- Candidates who are turned down often apply again after several months and a real change in their experience.
